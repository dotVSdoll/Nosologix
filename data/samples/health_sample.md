# Hypertension basics

Hypertension means blood pressure remains higher than the recommended range over time.

People with symptoms such as chest pain, severe headache, weakness on one side, or difficulty breathing should seek urgent medical care.

Lifestyle management often includes reducing salt intake, regular exercise, weight management, and following clinician guidance.

# Diabetes basics

Diabetes is a chronic condition where blood glucose remains higher than the recommended range because the body has trouble producing or using insulin.

Daily diabetes management may include checking blood glucose, following a nutrition plan, physical activity, and taking medicines exactly as directed by a clinician.

# Hydration basics

Hydration means getting enough fluids for normal body function. Water needs vary with activity level, weather, fever, vomiting, and diarrhea.

Mild dehydration can cause thirst, dry mouth, dark urine, tiredness, or dizziness. Severe dehydration can require urgent medical care.

# Lab report basics

Lab reports may mark values as elevated, low, abnormal, or outside a reference range. A single abnormal result should be interpreted with clinical context.

Patients should review important or abnormal lab results with a clinician, especially when symptoms are present or results are far outside the reference range.

# Medication safety

Medication dose decisions depend on age, weight, other medicines, allergies, pregnancy status, kidney function, liver function, and diagnosis.

Patients should not change prescription medication dosage without guidance from a licensed clinician or pharmacist.

# Pregnancy and child safety

Pregnant patients, infants, babies, and children need extra caution because symptoms and medication safety can differ from healthy adults.

Health questions involving pregnancy or children should be confirmed with a qualified clinician.
